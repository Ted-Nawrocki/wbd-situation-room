title: "Judge Questions the Settlement at Hearing; No Ruling Yet"
fact: "On September 24, 2026, Judge Martínez-Olguín held a virtual hearing on the proposed consent decree to address what her order called outstanding questions about its factual and legal underpinnings and implementation. She did not rule, saying a decision would come in due course. Hours before the hearing, she granted motions to file amicus briefs, including one from the opposition group Block the Merger, with a deadline of 12:01 a.m. Pacific on September 25. She asked the parties to respond by noon Pacific on Monday, September 28 to a letter from Senator Cory Booker, who urged an independent public-interest review, noting the decree reached the court without a competitive impact statement or public comment period. Asked how the decree addresses the states' competition concerns, counsel for the states said a block would be permanent and that the states chose remedies that address the harm without permanently changing the structure. Earlier in the week, the court vacated a September 24 hearing on Paramount's motion to require the states and the WGA to post a $1.88 billion bond. The merger has received approvals in more than 65 jurisdictions, and the FCC has approved a foreign ownership structure for the combined company."
context: "A settlement between the parties is not the end of the case. The court still has to enter the decree, and this judge has shown she will not treat approval as a formality. Every day of review now carries a price, since the ticking fee begins October 1. Watch the September 28 filings and the timing of any order: approval sets a closing date in motion; a request for more process pushes it out."
