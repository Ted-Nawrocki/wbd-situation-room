title: "Paramount Moves Its Listing to the NYSE and Sets the Warrant Record Date"
fact: "On September 25, 2026, Paramount Skydance filed a Form 8-K reporting two actions. Under Item 3.01(d), the board authorized a voluntary transfer of the Class B Common Stock listing from the Nasdaq Global Select Market to the New York Stock Exchange. Trading on Nasdaq is expected to end at market close on or about October 5, 2026, with NYSE trading beginning at market open on or about October 6, 2026. The filing gives no reason for the transfer. Under Item 7.01, the board set a record date of the close of business on October 5, 2026 for the previously announced distribution of warrants to Class B holders, with an expected issue date on or about October 13, 2026 and an expected ex-dividend date on or about October 14, 2026, the trading day after the issue date. Eligible holders receive one warrant per Class B share, rounded down; Paramount expects to issue approximately 470 million warrants. Lawrence J. Ellison, David F. Ellison, Gerald J. Cardinale, the Lawrence J. Ellison Revocable Trust, RedBird Capital Partners Fund IV, and their affiliates are excluded as Restricted Holders. Shares held by the Paramount Global 401(k) Plan and Master Trust receive Class B shares instead of warrants. Each warrant is exercisable for one Class B share at a price equal to the 20-trading-day VWAP ending on the third business day before the WBD closing, with a ceiling of $16.02 and a floor of $12.00, matching the price paid by the Equity Syndication Parties. The warrants have a 10-year term; after the third anniversary, Paramount may set an early expiration if the stock closes at or above $30.00 on 20 of any 30 consecutive trading days. Paramount intends to list the warrants on the NYSE. The warrant issuance and underlying shares will be registered on Form S-3. The distribution is contingent on the WBD closing, and Paramount may cancel or postpone the record date or issue date."
context: "This is a listing transfer, not a delisting for cause. The security, the holders, and the economics stay the same. What makes it matter is the timing: the record date falls on Nasdaq's last trading day, while the entitlement trading, the issue date, and the ex-date all happen on the NYSE. Because the ex-date follows the issue date, shares traded between October 6 and October 13 carry the warrant entitlement with them, which puts due bill tracking on top of a venue change. The schedule also tells you what Paramount expects: the issue date requires a prior closing, so Paramount is planning for the WBD deal to close on or before October 13, even though the court has not yet ruled on the state settlement."
