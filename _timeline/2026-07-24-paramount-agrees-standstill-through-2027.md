title: "Paramount Agrees Not to Close Until June 2027 or Five Days After Lawsuits Resolve"
fact: "On July 24, 2026, Paramount filed an agreement in federal court stating it will not take steps to close its acquisition of Warner Bros. Discovery until June 1, 2027, or five days after the lawsuits brought by the state attorneys general coalition and the Writers Guild of America are resolved, whichever comes first. As part of the agreement, the parties agreed to cancel the preliminary injunction hearing that had been scheduled for August 3, 2026, and to scrap the existing briefing schedule. The parties agreed to file a joint statement on trial scheduling by July 31, 2026. Paramount characterized the agreement as achieving what the states and the WGA had sought from the outset, a direct path to a trial based on the evidence. California Attorney General Rob Bonta stated the agreement was a significant win and that the states remain eager to prove their case. Shares of both Paramount and WBD declined on the news. The Paramount-WBD merger agreement has an outside date of March 4, 2027, which includes a one-time automatic extension to June 4, 2027 if all closing conditions other than regulatory approvals have been satisfied or waived."
context: "This is a materially larger delay than the TRO and its subsequent extension implied on their own. A TRO measured in weeks became a standstill measured in the better part of a year. It is also a voluntary agreement rather than a court order, which matters: Paramount chose this path over continuing to fight the injunction motion in the near term. Given the ticking fee accrues starting September 30 regardless of the litigation status, a standstill running into 2027 puts real, compounding cost on Paramount for as long as the cases take. At $650 million per quarter, a delay to June 2027 could add roughly $1.7 billion to what Paramount ultimately pays. The tradeoff Paramount is making is a bet that a fuller trial record, rather than an emergency motion, gives it the better chance of winning outright."
