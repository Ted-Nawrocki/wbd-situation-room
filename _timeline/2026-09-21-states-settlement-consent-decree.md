title: "Paramount and 12 States Settle; Proposed Five-Year Consent Decree Filed"
fact: "On September 21, 2026, California Attorney General Rob Bonta announced a proposed settlement with Paramount resolving the 12-state antitrust lawsuit. The states and the companies jointly asked the U.S. District Court for the Northern District of California to enter a consent decree and dissolve the order preventing the transaction from closing. The settlement requires court approval. The proposed decree runs through the end of the fifth calendar year after closing and contains no divestitures at signing. Key terms: at least an additional $300 million per year in U.S. film production, $1.5 billion over five years; at least 30 theatrical releases per year in years one and two and 32 in years three through five, with a 45-day theatrical window for wide releases; a $30 million contribution to guild health and retirement plans for each film short of the commitment, and potential divestiture of Paramount's Miramax interest for shortfalls; separate negotiation of Paramount and Warner Bros. basic cable channels, with potential divestiture of BET channels, VH1, Comedy Central, Smithsonian, Destination America, and Science for material violations; a prohibition on selling the Paramount or Warner Bros. studio lots in Los Angeles County during the term; a five-member News Editorial Independence Board for CBS News and CNN, to be formed within 180 days of closing; Pluto TV to remain free; a $5 million annual independent film fund; an internal compliance monitor, an independent monitoring trustee, and a five-state enforcement committee; and $40 million to reimburse the states' legal and expert costs. The Writers Guild of America, which had filed a separate suit, consented to the deal while stating it continues to oppose the merger. In a memo to staff, Paramount CEO David Ellison said he expected the transaction to close in about two weeks."
context: "Bonta had said repeatedly that only structural remedies would satisfy the states. The decree is behavioral, with divestiture held in reserve as a penalty rather than a condition. The timing is not a coincidence: the ticking fee starts accruing October 1, and a March trial would have run up several quarters of it. For corporate actions teams, the operative point is that closing has moved from a date tied to trial or June 2027 to a date that could arrive within weeks of court approval. The tender and exchange offers settle on that closing, so the unsettled window many desks had planned around may collapse suddenly."
