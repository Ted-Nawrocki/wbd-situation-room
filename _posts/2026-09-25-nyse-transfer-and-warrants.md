Paramount's September 25 Form 8-K reads like housekeeping. It is not. It schedules a change of listing venue and a 470 million warrant distribution in the same window, and ties both to a merger closing the court has not yet cleared.

## Just the facts

**Listing transfer (Item 3.01(d)):**
- Voluntary transfer of the Class B Common Stock from the Nasdaq Global Select Market to the NYSE
- Last Nasdaq trading day: on or about October 5, 2026
- First NYSE trading day: on or about October 6, 2026
- No reason given in the filing
- Paramount remains a "controlled company" under NYSE rules, exempt from certain governance requirements

**Warrant distribution (Item 7.01 and Exhibit 99):**
- Record date: close of business, October 5, 2026
- Issue date: on or about October 13, 2026, subject to the prior closing of the WBD acquisition
- Ex-date: on or about October 14, 2026, the trading day after the issue date
- One warrant per Class B share, rounded down; about 470 million warrants expected
- Excluded: the Ellisons, Gerald Cardinale, the Ellison Trust, RedBird, and affiliates; the Paramount Global 401(k) Plan receives Class B shares instead
- Exercise price: 20-day VWAP ending three business days before closing, between $12.00 and $16.02, the same price the Equity Syndication Parties pay
- Term: 10 years; after year three, Paramount may set an early expiration if the stock closes at or above $30.00 on 20 of 30 consecutive trading days
- Settlement: physical (cash) while the registration statement is effective; net share settlement if it is not
- If all 470 million are exercised for cash at $12.00, gross proceeds would be about $5.6 billion, for general corporate purposes
- Registered on Form S-3; warrants to be listed on the NYSE
- The distribution can be cancelled or postponed if the closing slips

## Ted's Takes

The record date sits on Nasdaq's last day, and everything after it happens on the NYSE. Because the ex-date follows the issue date, anyone who sells between October 6 and October 13 passes the warrant entitlement to the buyer, so due bills have to be tracked across a venue change, with primary exchange reference data flipping in the middle of the event. Add stock loans that owe warrants back to lenders, margin positions that may receive "in lieu of" credits instead of real warrants, and listed options likely moving to non-standard deliverables, and this is a busy week for any desk holding PSKY.

The schedule is also a signal. The issue date requires a closed deal, which means Paramount has built its calendar around closing by October 13. The court has not approved the settlement yet. If that slips, expect a cancelled or moved record date, announced under NYSE rules after October 5.
