The trial that was supposed to decide this deal in March is off the calendar, assuming the court agrees. On September 21, Paramount and the 12-state coalition filed a proposed five-year consent decree. On September 24, the judge questioned it and declined to rule on the spot.

## Just the facts

**The settlement (filed September 21, 2026):**
- Proposed consent decree running through the end of the fifth calendar year after closing
- No divestitures at signing; divestiture held in reserve as a penalty for material violations
- At least $300 million per year in additional U.S. film production, $1.5 billion over five years
- At least 30 theatrical releases per year in years one and two, 32 in years three through five, with a 45-day window for wide releases; $30 million to guild health and retirement plans for each film short, and potential divestiture of Paramount's Miramax interest
- Separate negotiation of Paramount and Warner Bros. basic cable channels; material violations could force divestiture of BET channels, VH1, Comedy Central, Smithsonian, Destination America, and Science
- No sale of the Paramount or Warner Bros. studio lots in Los Angeles County during the term
- A five-member News Editorial Independence Board for CBS News and CNN within 180 days of closing
- Independent monitoring trustee, internal compliance monitor, and a five-state enforcement committee
- $40 million to reimburse the states' legal and expert costs
- The WGA consented while stating it still opposes the merger

**The hearing (September 24, 2026):**
- The judge questioned the decree's legal and factual basis and did not rule
- She allowed amicus briefs from opponents, including Block the Merger
- She ordered responses to Senator Cory Booker's letter, which asks for a public-interest review, by noon Pacific on Monday, September 28

**The clock:**
- The ticking fee of $0.25 per share per quarter, about $7 million per day, begins accruing October 1
- David Ellison told staff he expects the deal to close in about two weeks

## Ted's Takes

The states traded a trial they might have won for five years of behavioral commitments with divestiture as the stick. Paramount traded several quarters of ticking fees for obligations it had mostly promised anyway. Both sides got certainty. The only party that has not agreed yet is the one that matters: the court.

For operations teams, the risk has flipped. For two months the problem was a closing that might not come until mid-2027. Now it is a closing that could arrive with little notice after an approval order. Settlement of Paramount's tender and exchange offers follows the closing. Desks that parked this file until spring should confirm they can process on short notice.
