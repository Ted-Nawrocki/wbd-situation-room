**As of October 1, 2026**

Ten months of headlines come down to one operational week.

## 1. The merger payout is not a round number
Each WBD share converts into $31.00 plus $0.00277778 per day after September 30. At an October 6 closing, that is $31.01666668 per share. Payment systems, client statements, and cost basis records all have to carry that figure precisely.

## 2. A listing venue changes mid-event
Paramount Class B shares are expected to leave Nasdaq on October 5 and begin trading on the NYSE on October 6. Reference data, closing auction routing, and index membership tied to the listing venue all change during the warrant entitlement period.

## 3. Warrants bring due bills
The record date is October 5, but the ex-date falls after the issue date. Shares sold between October 6 and October 13 carry the warrant entitlement to the buyer. Stock loans, margin positions, and listed options all need handling.

## 4. Note offers settle on the closing
Tendered and exchanged WBD notes have been waiting since May. Settlement follows the closing, converting short-dated notes to cash and longer-dated notes to Paramount second-lien secured notes for eligible holders.

## 5. New 144A paper enters the market
The acquisition notes were sold to QIBs under Rule 144A and to non-U.S. persons under Regulation S. Only the first lien notes carry registration rights, which points to a future registered exchange offer for those series.

## 6. Every desk is involved
Corporate actions, reference data, trading, securities lending, margin, options, fund accounting, tax, and client service all touch this closing. Transactions of this size rarely fail on strategy. They fail when execution breaks under complexity, and closing week is when that risk peaks.
