## Active Corporate Actions

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Closing Expected October 6, 2026 at $31.01666668 per WBD Share</div>
  <p>On October 1, 2026, Paramount and WBD announced the merger is expected to close on <strong>October 6, 2026</strong>, subject to customary closing conditions. Each WBD share converts into the right to receive $31.00 in cash plus $0.00277778 for each calendar day after September 30, 2026 through closing. At an October 6 closing, that is <strong>$31.01666668 per share</strong>.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Court Entered the Consent Decree on September 30, 2026</div>
  <p>Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín entered the five-year consent decree with the twelve plaintiff states and dissolved the order barring the closing, removing the last legal obstacle.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Listing Move and Warrants: October 5 to 14</div>
  <p>Paramount Class B shares are expected to leave Nasdaq at the close on or about October 5 and begin trading on the NYSE on or about October 6. October 5 is also the record date for about 470 million warrants, with an expected issue date of October 13 and ex-date of October 14. Shares traded between the record date and the ex-date carry the warrant entitlement.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">WBD Note Offers Settle on the Closing</div>
  <p>Paramount's cash tender offers (about $2.42 billion of 2027 and 2028 notes) and exchange offers (about $12.7 billion of longer-dated notes into Paramount second-lien secured notes) settle on or shortly after the closing.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Financing Priced</div>
  <p>On September 30, Paramount announced $41.4 billion and EUR 885 million of senior secured notes offerings and priced $8.5 billion and EUR 850 million of term loan B. The equity, up to $46.7 billion from the Ellison Trust and $250 million from RedBird, was syndicated in April 2026 to sovereign and institutional investors, with Larry Ellison's guarantee as the backstop.</p>
</div>

## Transaction Summary

Paramount Skydance Corporation is the winning bidder. The contest for WBD ended on February 27, 2026. As of October 1, 2026, every regulatory and legal condition is resolved, the acquisition financing is priced, and closing is expected October 6, 2026.

## Cast of characters

### Warner Bros. Discovery

**David Zaslav**, President and Chief Executive Officer. Steered the separation plan and the contested sale process.

**Gunnar Wiedenfels**, Chief Financial Officer. Architect of the June 2025 <span class="glossary-term" data-term="liability-management">liability management</span> framework and the May 2026 consent solicitations.

**Samuel A. Di Piazza, Jr.**, Chair of the Board. Led the Superior Proposal determination.

### Netflix

**Ted Sarandos** and **Greg Peters**, Co-Chief Executive Officers. Declined to match on February 27, 2026.

### Paramount Skydance

**David Ellison**, Chairman and Chief Executive Officer. Drove the whole-company bid and negotiated the state settlement.

**Larry Ellison**, Oracle co-founder and, with David Ellison and RedBird, a holder of Paramount's voting Class A shares. Personal guarantor of the Ellison Trust's equity commitment of up to $46.7 billion, now syndicated to outside investors.

**Makan Delrahim**, Chief Legal Officer. Former head of the DOJ Antitrust Division.

**Jeffrey Kessler**, lead outside counsel in the state litigation.

### Regulators and courts

**DOJ Antitrust Division.** Closed its investigation without conditions on June 12, 2026.

**State Attorneys General.** Twelve states led by California's Rob Bonta sued July 13, 2026; their consent decree was entered September 30, 2026.

**U.S. District Court, Northern District of California.** Judge Araceli Martínez-Olguín granted the July TRO and entered the consent decree on September 30, 2026.

**European Commission.** Cleared the deal July 22, 2026 with a film distribution remedy.

This page tells the story in episodes. **Just the facts** are kept clean. **Ted's Takes** connect the dots.

---

## Episode 1: The origin story

### Just the facts
- In May 2021, AT&T announced a transaction to combine WarnerMedia with Discovery. It closed in April 2022.

### Ted's Takes
The balance sheet was cast in a lead role from the pilot.

---

## Episode 2: Living with the balance sheet

### Just the facts
- WBD prioritized cost reduction, free cash flow, and deleveraging while linear networks declined and streaming competition intensified.

### Ted's Takes
When a company is constrained, the options that survive are the ones the capital structure allows.

---

## Episode 3: June 2025, the refinancing that paved the runway

### Just the facts
- WBD announced plans to separate into Streaming and Studios and Global Networks, supported by a broad debt refinancing and <span class="glossary-term" data-term="liability-management">liability management</span> effort.

### Ted's Takes
Netflix and Paramount did not build the runway. They showed up once the lights were on.

---

## Episode 4: The bidders enter

### Just the facts
- Netflix agreed to buy Streaming and Studios after separation at $27.75/share. Paramount launched an all-cash <span class="glossary-term" data-term="tender-offer">tender offer</span> for the whole company at $30.00/share.

### Ted's Takes
Cash for everything versus buy the growth engine after reorganizing the stage.

---

## Episode 5: Delaware and the structural critique

### Just the facts
- Paramount sued in Delaware Chancery on January 13, 2026; the motion to expedite was denied January 15.

### Ted's Takes
Litigation, a tender offer, and a proxy contest, all running at once.

---

## Episode 6: Netflix goes all cash

### Just the facts
- On January 20, 2026, Netflix converted its offer to all cash at $27.75 per share.

### Ted's Takes
Netflix removed Paramount's best structural argument and forced the comparison back to price.

---

## Episode 7: The Superior Proposal

### Just the facts
- On February 26, 2026, after a Netflix waiver, the WBD board declared Paramount's $31.00 offer a Superior Proposal.

### Ted's Takes
One dollar, plus no separation, gave the board a real fiduciary basis to switch.

---

## Episode 8: Netflix exits, Paramount signs

### Just the facts
- On February 27, 2026, Netflix declined to match and WBD signed with Paramount at $31.00 per share.

### Ted's Takes
"Declining to match" framed the exit as a choice, and the economics supported it.

---

## Episode 9: The shareholders speak

### Just the facts
- On April 23, 2026, shareholders approved the merger, 1.7 billion for and about 16.3 million against.

### Ted's Takes
A formality confirming what institutions had already decided.

---

## Episode 10: The debt management curtain call

### Just the facts
- On May 19, 2026, WBD's subsidiary issuers launched consent solicitations across 17 note series, and Paramount launched tender offers on two short-dated series and exchange offers on thirteen longer-dated series.
- Requisite consents were received May 26; the consent payment funded around May 29.
- The tender and exchange offers settle on the acquisition closing.

### Ted's Takes
The consent leg cleared cleanly. The offers it unlocked have been waiting on a courtroom ever since.

---

## Episode 11: Cleared and blocked

### Just the facts
- DOJ closed its investigation June 12, 2026. Twelve states sued July 13. A TRO issued July 20. The EU cleared July 22. On July 24, Paramount agreed not to close before June 1, 2027 or five days after the litigation resolved. On August 4, trial was set for March 2 to 19, 2027.

### Ted's Takes
Federal clearance and a state injunction answered different legal questions. Only one of them controlled the calendar.

---

## Episode 12: The settlement

### Just the facts
- On September 21, 2026, Paramount and the states filed a proposed five-year consent decree with behavioral commitments on film output, U.S. production spending, cable negotiations, studio lots, and news independence, with divestiture as a penalty for material violations. The WGA consented.
- On September 24, the judge questioned the decree, allowed amicus briefs, and ordered responses to Senator Booker's letter by September 28. She has not ruled.
- The ticking fee begins October 1, 2026.

### Ted's Takes
The states traded a winnable trial for five years of monitored commitments. Paramount traded quarters of ticking fees for promises it had largely made already. The remaining signature belongs to the court, and this judge has shown she will not treat it as a formality.

---

## Episode 13: The money has to show up

### Just the facts
- The Ellison Trust committed up to $46.7 billion and RedBird $250 million in new Class B shares, subject to increase if ticking consideration becomes payable.
- In April 2026, the full amount was syndicated to affiliates of the Ellisons and RedBird, the Public Investment Fund, L'Imad, QIA TMT Holding, and LionTree. The syndication does not release the original commitments, and Larry Ellison's guarantee, including an obligation to add equity for the lenders' solvency certificate, remains in force.
- Per an early 2026 SEC filing, the Trust held about 1.16 billion Oracle shares, roughly 30% pledged against personal loans. Oracle closed at $137.10 on September 25, 2026, about 60% below its September 2025 peak. On September 12, Larry Ellison canceled a plan to sell up to $7.5 billion of Oracle stock a day after it was disclosed; no shares were sold.

### Ted's Takes
Sovereign money now sits in front of the Ellisons. Their Oracle-heavy balance sheet is the backstop, called on only if a syndication party fails to fund, the solvency certificate needs more equity, or ticking consideration grows the check. Backstops matter most when something else has already gone wrong, which is why this one deserves attention.

---

## Episode 14: Changing stages mid-scene

### Just the facts
- On September 25, 2026, Paramount announced a voluntary transfer of its Class B listing from Nasdaq to the NYSE, with Nasdaq trading ending on or about October 5 and NYSE trading starting on or about October 6.
- The same filing set October 5 as the record date for about 470 million warrants, issue date on or about October 13, ex-date on or about October 14, all contingent on the WBD closing.
- Warrants carry the syndication price ($12.00 to $16.02), a 10-year term, and a possible early expiration after year three if the stock holds $30.00. They will be registered on Form S-3 and listed on the NYSE.

### Ted's Takes
The record date lands on Nasdaq's last day; the entitlement trading, issue date, and ex-date all land on the NYSE. With the ex-date after the issue date, due bills follow every trade from October 6 through October 13. And because the issue date requires a closed deal, Paramount has effectively told the market it expects to close by October 13, before the court has ruled.

---

## Episode 15: Final cut

### Just the facts
- On September 30, 2026, the court entered the consent decree and dissolved the order barring the closing.
- The same day, Paramount announced $41.4 billion and EUR 885 million of senior secured notes offerings and priced $8.5 billion and EUR 850 million of term loan B.
- On October 1, 2026, the companies announced an expected closing of October 6, 2026, with WBD holders receiving $31.00 plus $0.00277778 per day after September 30, or $31.01666668 per share at an October 6 closing.

### Ted's Takes
After ten months, the ending is a date and a number. For operations teams, October 6 starts the busiest week of the deal: the merger payout to eight decimals, a new primary listing venue for Paramount, a warrant entitlement period with due bills, and settlement of the WBD note offers that have waited since May.
