## Active Corporate Actions

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Merger on Hold Until June 2027 or Five Days After Lawsuits Resolve</div>
  <p>Following the July 20, 2026 TRO, Paramount agreed on July 24, 2026 not to take steps to close the acquisition until <strong>June 1, 2027</strong>, or five days after the state attorneys general and Writers Guild of America lawsuits are resolved, whichever comes first. The August 3 preliminary injunction hearing was cancelled by agreement. A joint statement on trial scheduling is due July 31, 2026. The acquisition cannot close, and settlement of Paramount's tender and exchange offers cannot occur, until the standstill lifts.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">DOJ and EU Have Both Cleared the Deal</div>
  <p>The DOJ Antitrust Division closed its investigation without conditions on June 12, 2026. The European Commission cleared the deal on July 22, 2026, conditioned on Paramount exiting a film distribution joint venture with Universal in Europe. Neither clearance binds the state court proceeding in California, which is evaluating different markets under different legal standards.</p>
</div>

<div class="callout">
  <div class="callout-title">Ticking Fee Accrues September 30, 2026 Regardless of the Standstill</div>
  <p>If the transaction has not closed by September 30, 2026, Paramount owes WBD shareholders a ticking fee of $0.25 per share per quarter, approximately $650 million per quarter, accruing for as long as the standstill runs. A delay to June 2027 could add roughly $1.7 billion to the deal price. If the deal fails entirely for regulatory reasons, Paramount owes WBD a $7 billion reverse termination fee.</p>
</div>

## Transaction Summary

Paramount Skydance Corporation is the winning bidder. The contest for WBD ended on February 27, 2026. As of July 24, 2026, the transaction is federally and internationally cleared but is on hold under a standstill agreement through June 2027 or five days after pending state and labor union litigation resolves, whichever comes first.

## Cast of characters

These transactions are executed by people, not abstractions. Understanding who is making decisions, and from what seat, matters as much as understanding the documents themselves.

### Warner Bros. Discovery

**David Zaslav**  
President and Chief Executive Officer  
The central figure in this story. Oversaw the separation strategy, balance sheet decisions, and the board's navigation of a contested bidding process that ultimately produced a better outcome for shareholders than the original Netflix transaction.

**Gunnar Wiedenfels**  
Chief Financial Officer  
Architect of the June 2025 <span class="glossary-term" data-term="liability-management">liability management</span> and separation framework. Now overseeing the debt management process that remains conditioned on a closing the courts have not yet permitted.

**Samuel A. Di Piazza, Jr.**  
Chair of the Board of Directors  
Led the board through the Superior Proposal determination process, the Netflix waiver negotiation, and the transition from contested situation to signed deal.

---

### Netflix

**Ted Sarandos**  
Co-Chief Executive Officer  
Announced the decision to decline to match Paramount's $31.00/share bid on February 27, 2026.

**Greg Peters**  
Co-Chief Executive Officer  
Jointly announced the withdrawal with Sarandos, framing the decision as a capital discipline call rather than a loss.

---

### Paramount Skydance

**David Ellison**  
Chairman and Chief Executive Officer  
Driving force behind the all-cash, whole-company acquisition thesis. Now defending the transaction in federal court against a 12-state antitrust coalition.

**Makan Delrahim**  
Chief Legal Officer  
Former head of the DOJ Antitrust Division under the first Trump administration, recruited by Ellison in 2025. Led the public response to the European Commission's clearance.

**Jeffrey Kessler**  
Lead outside counsel representing Paramount in the state attorneys general litigation.

---

### Regulators and courts

**U.S. Department of Justice -- Antitrust Division**  
Closed its investigation without conditions on June 12, 2026, after an eight-month review.

**State Attorneys General Coalition**  
Twelve states led by California's Rob Bonta filed suit July 13, 2026, alleging Clayton Act violations. The coalition includes Arizona, California, Colorado, Connecticut, Massachusetts, Minnesota, Nevada, New Jersey, New Mexico, New York, Oregon, and Washington.

**U.S. District Court, Northern District of California**  
Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin is presiding over the state coalition's lawsuit and granted the temporary restraining order now in effect.

**European Commission**  
Cleared the transaction July 22, 2026, subject to a film distribution remedy.

This is one of those rare corporate actions stories that plays like prestige TV: iconic studios, streaming giants, a leveraged capital structure, and a contested process where deadlines and documentation were part of the weaponry.

This page tells the story in episodes.  
**Just the facts** are kept clean.  
**Ted's Takes** are where I connect the dots, professionally, and with a little knowing wit.

---

## Episode 1: The origin story

### Just the facts
- In May 2021, AT&T announced a transaction to separate WarnerMedia and combine it with Discovery.
- The transaction closed in April 2022, creating Warner Bros. Discovery as a publicly traded company.

### Ted's Takes
This is the pilot episode where the audience thinks the stars are the characters on screen. In reality, the balance sheet is cast in a lead role.

---

## Episode 2: Living with the balance sheet

### Just the facts
- After formation, WBD emphasized cost reduction, free cash flow, and deleveraging.
- The business operated against two opposing backdrops: structural pressure in linear networks and relentless competition in streaming.

### Ted's Takes
When a company is constrained, the options that survive are the ones the capital structure allows.

---

## Episode 3: June 2025, the refinancing that paved the runway

### Just the facts
- In June 2025, WBD announced plans to separate into two publicly traded companies: **Streaming & Studios** and **Global Networks**.
- WBD pursued a broad debt refinancing and <span class="glossary-term" data-term="liability-management">liability management</span> effort supported by committed bridge financing.

### Ted's Takes
June 2025 paved the runway. Netflix and Paramount did not invent it. They showed up once the lights were on.

---

## Episode 4: The bidders enter

### Just the facts
- Netflix announced a definitive transaction to acquire Warner Bros. (Streaming & Studios) following the separation, at $27.75/share.
- Paramount launched an all-cash <span class="glossary-term" data-term="tender-offer">tender offer</span> for the entire company at $30.00/share.

### Ted's Takes
Paramount's pitch: cash for everything. Netflix's pitch: buy the growth engine after the company reorganizes the stage.

---

## Episode 5: Delaware Court and the structural critique

### Just the facts
- Paramount filed suit in Delaware Chancery Court on January 13, 2026, alleging breach of fiduciary duty.
- Vice Chancellor Morgan T. Zurn denied Paramount's motion to expedite on January 15, 2026.
- Paramount announced plans to nominate directors for WBD's 2026 annual meeting.

### Ted's Takes
This is where the plot shifts from competing offers to parallel campaigns: litigation, a tender offer, and a proxy contest running at once.

---

## Episode 6: Netflix eliminates the structural argument

### Just the facts
- On January 20, 2026, Netflix and WBD amended their merger agreement to an all-cash structure at $27.75 per share.
- WBD filed its preliminary proxy statement the same day, targeting an April 2026 shareholder vote.

### Ted's Takes
Netflix removed Paramount's best argument, the complexity of a cash-and-stock deal, and forced the comparison back to price.

---

## Episode 7: The Superior Proposal

### Just the facts
- On February 26, 2026, after a contractual waiver from Netflix, WBD's board reopened negotiations with Paramount.
- The board determined Paramount's revised $31.00 per share offer constituted a Superior Proposal under the Netflix agreement.

### Ted's Takes
The move from $30.00 to $31.00, combined with no separation required, gave the board a real fiduciary basis to reconsider.

---

## Episode 8: Netflix Exits, Paramount Signs

### Just the facts
- On February 27, 2026, Netflix declined to match. WBD and Paramount signed a definitive Agreement and Plan of Merger at $31.00 per share.
- Enterprise value approximately $110.9 billion. Merger Agreement end date March 4, 2027, subject to extension.

### Ted's Takes
"Declining to match" is precise language. Netflix framed the exit as a choice, and the framing was defensible given the economics.

---

## Episode 9: The Shareholders Speak

### Just the facts
- On April 23, 2026, WBD shareholders approved the merger, 1.7 billion votes in favor against approximately 16.3 million against.
- Shareholders did not approve the executive compensation package presented at the same meeting.

### Ted's Takes
That vote was a formality confirming a conclusion institutional investors had already reached well before the meeting.

---

## Episode 10: The Debt Management Curtain Call

### Just the facts
- On May 19, 2026, WBD's subsidiary issuers launched consent solicitations across 17 note series. Paramount concurrently launched tender offers on two short-dated series ($2.4 billion cap) and exchange offers on thirteen longer-dated series ($12.8 billion cap).
- On May 27, 2026, Paramount announced requisite consents received across all three indentures. Supplemental indentures were executed May 26, with the consent payment funded around May 29.
- The tender and exchange offers remain open, with settlement conditioned on the acquisition closing.

### Ted's Takes
The consent leg cleared cleanly. The offers it unlocked are now waiting on a courtroom in Oakland, not just a regulatory calendar.

---

## Episode 11: Cleared and Blocked

### Just the facts
- On June 12, 2026, the DOJ closed its investigation without conditions after an eight-month review.
- On July 13, 2026, 12 states led by California sued to block the deal, alleging Clayton Act violations in theatrical distribution and cable licensing. The WGA filed a related suit the following day.
- On July 20, 2026, Judge Araceli Martinez-Olguin granted a temporary restraining order halting the closing.
- On July 22, 2026, the European Commission cleared the deal, conditioned on Paramount exiting a film distribution joint venture with Universal in Europe.
- On July 24, 2026, Paramount agreed not to close the transaction until June 1, 2027, or five days after the state and WGA lawsuits resolve, whichever comes first. The parties cancelled the scheduled preliminary injunction hearing and agreed to file a joint trial scheduling statement by July 31, 2026.
- The merger agreement's outside date is March 4, 2027, with a one-time automatic extension to June 4, 2027 if all conditions other than regulatory approvals are satisfied or waived. A ticking fee of $0.25 per share per quarter begins accruing to WBD shareholders starting September 30, 2026, regardless of the standstill. If the deal fails entirely for regulatory reasons, Paramount owes WBD a $7 billion reverse termination fee.

### Ted's Takes
Federal clearance and a state court fight are not contradictory outcomes. They are two different bodies answering two different legal questions, and the standstill Paramount agreed to on July 24 shows which one currently controls the calendar.

The scale of the change is worth sitting with. Four days separated a 14-day TRO from a standstill that can run to June 2027. Paramount chose that path voluntarily, favoring a full trial record over a rushed emergency hearing. The economics explain why: at $650 million per quarter in ticking fees against a $7 billion reverse termination fee, Paramount can absorb close to eleven quarters of delay before the cost of waiting equals the cost of losing outright. That math points toward litigating hard rather than settling early.

For anyone tracking the paper: settlement of Paramount's tender and exchange offers is conditioned on the acquisition closing, and the standstill means that condition will not be satisfied for a long stretch. Positions tendered into those offers now sit in an unsettled state that could last the better part of a year.

## What to watch next

**Active now (July 2026):**
- Standstill agreement in effect: no closing before June 1, 2027 or five days after the state and WGA lawsuits resolve, whichever is earlier
- Joint statement on trial scheduling due July 31, 2026
- Settlement of Paramount's tender and exchange offers remains conditioned on closing, which the standstill currently prohibits

**Pending:**
- Trial scheduling and the litigation timeline for both the state coalition and WGA cases
- Ticking fee accrual beginning September 30, 2026, continuing for the duration of the delay
- Confirmed closing date, contingent on the litigation's resolution or the June 2027 outside window

**Structural questions that remain open:**
- Whether the cases proceed to trial or reach a negotiated resolution before then
- How the combined company's post-close capital structure and synergy targets hold up given a closing potentially a year or more later than originally targeted
- Whether the accumulated ticking fee changes either party's calculus on settlement
