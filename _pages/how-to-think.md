*Updated October 1, 2026. Every regulatory and legal condition is resolved, the financing is priced, and closing is expected October 6, 2026.*

### 1. A signed deal is not a closed deal, until it is.
Between February and September, this transaction cleared shareholders, the DOJ, the European Commission, and finally a federal court. Each step removed one risk and exposed the next. The lesson for practitioners: track every gating condition separately, because they resolve on separate clocks.

### 2. Federal clearance does not bind the states.
The DOJ cleared the deal without conditions in June. Twelve states sued anyway in July and held up the closing for more than two months. State attorneys general have independent antitrust authority, and this deal showed they will use it.

### 3. June 2025 built the runway.
WBD's separation plan and liability management defined which assets could move together and cleared covenant and maturity obstacles. The bidders arrived after the conditions for a sale were in place.

### 4. Price decides contests; structure decides closings.
Paramount won with $31.00 for the whole company. Everything after that, from the ticking fee to the consent decree to the debt pricing, was about structure, timing, and conditions.

### 5. Follow the financing to the closing date.
The equity was syndicated to sovereign and institutional investors, with Larry Ellison's guarantee as the backstop. The debt was priced the week before closing. Backstops and solvency certificates matter most on the day funds settle.

### 6. This is a corporate actions story wearing an M&A headline.
The final week stacks a cash merger payout, a change of listing venue, a warrant distribution with due bills, and settlement of tender and exchange offers on the same calendar. Strategy set the direction. Operations decide whether it lands cleanly.
